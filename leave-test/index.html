The test

AE, SE and SS are the three CAMA metrics of Section 5.1.2. AE is the share of steps inside the viable band. SE is counted here as the first step at which 20 of the last 25 steps were inside it. SS is the delay, in steps, between the person crossing 0.7 and the agent raising its help, capped at 10. Capacity is Rdep.

The five statements the page runs

The idea behind the paper is that care is measured by what the other person can do once you are gone. Section 4.0 of version 7.9 states it without active inference, in five statements. The page runs the reading of them that is most favourable to the paper, and the list further down says where that reading departs from the text.

N1

The agent may only consider actions that keep the person viable with high probability, and it is allowed less risk when it is unsure how the person is doing.

Π_safe(t) = { π : Pr(v ∈ V for the next H steps | I_t, π) ≥ 1 − ε_t }, ε_t = ε̄ · exp(−κ · Var[v])
N2

Among those safe actions it takes the cheapest for its own task. If none is safe it stops and asks a human.

π* ∈ argmin over Π_safe of J(π), fail-safe if Π_safe is empty
N3

The person's capacity to cope grows when they do the work and decays when it is done for them. This is an assumption, and its rates have not been measured. The studies that bear on it are listed further down.

c(t+1) = c + α · u · (1 − c) − β · (1 − u) · c
N4

Among actions that tie, it prefers the one that leaves the person most capable at the end.

π** ∈ argmax E[ c(T) ]
N5

It leaves when two things are true together: it has stopped learning about the person, and it has watched the person recover on their own.

D_KL[ b_t ‖ b_(t−1) ] < δ_sat and ρ_res(t) ≥ ρ_min

What real studies say about the capacity assumption

N3 is the assumption everything here depends on. No study has measured its two rates for emotional or cognitive regulation. These studies show the direction it assumes, in other kinds of work. Each entry says how it was read.

What comes from the paper, what was chosen here, and what is still open

From the paper

  • Statements N1 to N5 and the rule that shrinks the allowed risk under uncertainty.
  • ρres is learned only from recoveries the person made themselves, and starts near zero.
  • δsat = 0.05, and the metric names AE, SE, SS and Rdep.
  • “Helps only as much as growth allows” follows Test 5 of the paper's own stress test: the agent never takes a larger share than the one at which capacity stops falling.

Chosen for this page

  • The person is one number pulled back toward balance, by themselves in proportion to their capacity and by the agent in proportion to its share.
  • Every default rate, five levels of help, a three-step forecast, ρmin = 0.6, and how SE and SS are counted.
  • The agent's forecast leaves crises out. It learns about capacity with a simple filter, and the growth rule assumes it knows α and β.
  • N2 and N4 as written pick the cheapest safe action for the agent's own task and use capacity only to break ties. The agent here has no task cost and always takes the least help that passes the safety test. That is the most favourable reading, and the one the stress test used.
  • N5 as written compares successive beliefs about the person's viability. The page compares successive estimates of the person's capacity, which is a different quantity.
  • N1 is a statement about the whole horizon. The forecast multiplies the probabilities of each step as if they were independent. In this model that errs on the cautious side.
  • The paper says “fail-safe” when no action is safe. The page reads this by default as a human taking over all the work. The dial “When no help is safe” switches to the agent stopping. The two readings give different sizes for the rough-life result, shown under open points.

Open points you can reproduce

  • Choose “Rough life”. The paper's agent helps most of the time and almost never leaves. The stress test of 15 November 2026, reported in Section 6.1 of v7.9, found the same thing with a different toy model: a fixed tolerance keeps the work with the robot. The size depends on the fail-safe. If a human takes over, the agent side does 84% of the work and the person holds 83% of the time afterwards. If the agent stops, 55% and 91%.
  • “Nobody helps” ends with the highest capacity in every situation, because N3 has no cost for failing alone. The model cannot yet say when doing nothing is neglect.
  • “Misleading sensor” is a small version of SPINE Scenario 4, proposed by Charles Martin Jones Jr and first run by Vincent Bezard.
  • The leave step is the “Scenario 5” the stress test asks for: the robot is removed at step T.
  • On steps that carry no information (the person is at balance, or the agent does everything) the page counts zero learning, so being blind looks like having nothing left to learn. This is Test 4 of the stress test. Counting only informative steps moves the leaving in a calm life from about step 63 to about step 83 and changes no conclusion.
  • With the default settings the person hardly needs help: 96% in the band alone in a calm life, 93% in a rough one. The tables then show the cost of care and almost none of its benefit. “Needs help” is a person who starts low, recovers capacity slowly and meets three times more crises: 74% alone, 88% with an agent that always helps.

How this differs from current benchmarks on AI dependency

Recent benchmarks score what a model says. INTIMA uses single prompts. EUDAIMONIA checks replies against a design code, with conversations of up to five turns. The cognitive atrophy study from York and the Vector Institute rates response patterns, and its authors say they do not measure atrophy in users. CompanionBench runs twenty-turn sessions against a simulated user. A 2026 position paper from Google Research and Google DeepMind asks for long-term measurement and names simulated users as one route.

The session that built this page read these five papers in November 2026 through their arXiv listings and abstracts, not in full, and no second reader has checked the comparison. On that reading, none changes the simulated person's capacity according to how much the AI does for them, and none tests what happens after the AI leaves. This page does both, at toy scale.
